An open protocol for human connection.

Kindling lets people host their own profiles anywhere on the web and find each other through curated Pools. The introduction is the product. The introduction does its job and disappears. No one in the middle has a financial reason to keep two people apart.

v0.1 published April 2026. Apache 2.0 (code) and CC BY 4.0 (spec).

Why this exists

Two of the most important categories of human connection (dating and friendship) have been broken by the same business model. Centralized matchmaking apps make money on the wait, never on the meeting. Friendship discovery is worse: no category-defining product exists for it because friendship is hard to monetize the way dating is.

People are already routing around this in public. Dating docs on Google. Friendship spreadsheets on Notion. Personal pages on Carrd that read like dating profiles. The behavior already exists. What is missing is a way to search, scale, and connect across these self-hosted profiles without rebuilding the trap.

Kindling is the layer that lets the existing behavior work as infrastructure.

Two stacked open standards

Layer 1

Profile standard

A profile is whatever a person publishes at a URL. The protocol parses what is there. IndieWeb microformats (h-card) are the baseline; an AI parser fills in whatever the page does not mark up. Kindling stores no profile data.

Layer 2

Pool standard

A Pool is a manifest of profile entries grouped by context. A city, a scene, an interest, a relationship orientation. Anyone can create a Pool. Profiles are added only with the owner's consent, through a handshake.

Any UI a developer wants to build can sit on top: a swipe-style client, a list view, a search box, a Reddit-style browse, a Craigslist-style index. The two layers underneath stay the same.

How a connection happens

  1. Sarah hosts a profile on her Notion page.
  2. A curator (Mira) submits Sarah's URL to her Pool.
  3. The Pool sends Sarah a handshake message.
  4. Sarah accepts. Her structured profile is now part of the Pool.
  5. Reza queries the Pool: "who is up for a hike this weekend?"
  6. Sarah's profile surfaces. Reza opens her Notion page, decides to reach out.
  7. They exchange messages. The protocol disappears.

Native messaging is delivered as structured email under the hood, so spam filtering inherits decades of work for free, and no one has to host an inbox. Implementations render conversations however they want.

Sustainability without extraction

The protocol is free. The connection layer is free. Future versions of the framework will introduce optional, post-result revenue surfaces (gratitude offerings after a meaningful connection, the Hall of Flame for celebrating life partnerships) but the connection layer itself stays free, in every implementation, forever. The economic relationship in Kindling happens after a connection has succeeded, never before.

Built for several audiences

For builders

Fork the spec. Build a Kindling client. The economics of platform vs. protocol favor variety.

For writers

Cover the protocol's launch. The press list is small and curated; there's room.

For curators

Run a Pool. Curate it well. The early curators define what good Pool curation looks like.

For people

The first generation of Kindling clients will be rough. Watch what happens here over the next year.
