# Code of Conduct

*Kindling project Code of Conduct. Version 0.1, April 2026.*

The Kindling project builds infrastructure for human connection. The way we treat each other while building it has to live up to that.

This document describes the standards we hold each other to, the scope of where they apply, the process for reporting violations, and the consequences for behavior that falls outside them.

---

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in the Kindling project a respectful experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, and healthy community.

---

## Our standards

**Behavior we expect:**

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, then learning from the experience
- Focusing on what is best for the broader community, the people the protocol serves, and the long-term durability of the project

**Behavior we will not tolerate:**

- Harassment in any form, including comments related to identity, appearance, or any of the categories listed in the pledge above
- Sexualized language or imagery, and any sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, deliberately provocative comments, personal attacks, or political flame bait
- Public or private harassment, including unwelcome contact, doxing, or repeated unwanted attention
- Publishing others' private information without their explicit permission
- Sustained disruption of project discussions, RFCs, or events
- Coordinated brigading or pile-ons, including from outside the community
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

**Behavior that is specifically out of bounds for a project like this one:**

Because Kindling is infrastructure for human connection, certain categories of behavior are taken especially seriously:

- Using project channels (issues, discussions, chat) to solicit personal contact, romantic interest, or relationships from other contributors
- Using a Pool, the registry, or any reference implementation to identify, target, or harass any person
- Submitting profiles to Pools without consent (the consent handshake exists for a reason; circumventing it is grounds for immediate removal)
- Building implementations or running Pools designed to surveil, scrape, or aggregate profile data outside the protocol's consent model
- Targeting any subset of the community based on identity for any reason

This is not an exhaustive list. The principle is simple: if you're using the project to do something to another person without their consent, you are working against what we're building.

---

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all spaces operated by the Kindling project:

- The GitHub repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, code comments)
- The public registry's submission and review process
- The Kindling chat channels (when established)
- Project-affiliated events, virtual or in-person
- Any space where someone is officially representing the project (speaking at a conference under the project's name, posting on social media in an official capacity)

It also applies to behavior **outside** the project's spaces when that behavior:

- Targets a contributor or community member because of their participation in the project
- Causes harm to other contributors that meaningfully affects their ability to participate

We don't claim jurisdiction over your unrelated public life. We do reserve the right to take action when conduct elsewhere demonstrably affects the safety or function of this community.

---

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, report it to the **Code of Conduct Committee** at:

**conduct@kindling.dev**

All reports are reviewed by the committee. Reports are confidential to the committee unless the reporter explicitly authorizes broader disclosure. The committee will not retaliate against anyone who makes a report in good faith, and will not share the reporter's identity with the reported party without consent.

What to include in a report (none of these are required, but they help):

- What happened (as factually as you can describe it)
- When and where it happened
- Who was involved
- Any record of the incident (screenshots, links, logs)
- What outcome you'd like to see, if you have a preference

The committee aims to acknowledge reports within 72 hours and to resolve them within 30 days. Complex situations may take longer; you will be informed if so.

## The Code of Conduct Committee

The committee is composed of three people who are **not** maintainers of the spec or the reference implementation. This separation is intentional. People who hold technical commit authority should not also hold disciplinary authority over the people contributing to that authority.

The first committee was named at the v0.1 launch. The current committee members are listed at the end of this file. New committee members are nominated by the existing committee and confirmed by the Working Group (per GOVERNANCE.md). Members serve two-year terms. No member may serve more than two consecutive terms.

The committee has the authority to:

- Investigate reports
- Request information from the reported party
- Recommend or impose consequences (see "Consequences" below)
- Refer matters to law enforcement when there is evidence of unlawful activity (with the reporter's consent, except in cases involving imminent harm)

---

## Consequences

The committee chooses consequences proportionate to the violation and to any pattern of prior behavior. The available consequences are:

**1. Private warning.** A direct message from the committee describing the behavior, why it violated the Code of Conduct, and a request to change.

**2. Public warning.** A public note (in the relevant channel or repository) that the behavior violated the Code of Conduct, with no further restrictions.

**3. Temporary suspension.** Removal from one or more project channels for a defined period (typically 7-90 days). The suspended person can return after the period ends.

**4. Indefinite suspension.** Removal from all project channels for an indefinite period, with the door open to revisit.

**5. Permanent ban.** Removal from all project channels with no path to return. Reserved for the most serious violations or for repeated patterns after lesser consequences have been tried.

**Specific to the protocol's surfaces:**

- A Pool curator who violates the Code of Conduct may have their Pool's listing suspended or removed from the public registry.
- An implementation that violates the Code of Conduct (for example, an implementation that ignores the consent handshake) may be de-listed from the registry.
- A profile shown to be operated by someone using Kindling for harassment may be added to the shared block list.

These protocol-surface consequences are separate from individual-participation consequences. A person can be banned from the GitHub repository while their Pool remains listed, or vice versa, depending on what the violation actually involved.

---

## Appeals

A person who has been disciplined under this Code of Conduct can appeal the decision once, within 30 days of being informed. Appeals go to the Working Group (per GOVERNANCE.md). The Working Group reviews the committee's process and the proportionality of the consequence. The Working Group can uphold, reduce, or (rarely) overturn the committee's decision. The Working Group cannot increase the consequence on appeal.

---

## A note on disagreement vs. harm

Strong disagreement is not a violation of this Code of Conduct. People will disagree about the spec, about implementation choices, about governance, about messaging, about names. That's how a protocol gets better. Reviewers are expected to push back. Authors are expected to defend their work. RFCs that get rejected are still valuable; the rejection is the project working as designed.

This Code of Conduct exists to address harm, not to suppress disagreement. The committee draws the line where behavior moves from "I think you're wrong about this" to "I am acting against your ability to participate." That distinction matters.

If you're unsure whether something crosses the line, you can email `conduct@kindling.dev` for a private read before escalating publicly.
